# Game Design

Create a complete, highly polished **retro 2D/2.5D fighting game inspired by classic Tekken**, using the **El Camino Real Math Department** as the theme for the fighters.

The game should feel like an authentic old-school fighting game that could have existed on an arcade cabinet or early console, but with surprisingly deep and responsive combat. Do NOT make it feel like a generic modern mobile fighting game or a simple arcade brawler.

## VISUAL STYLE

Use a heavily pixelated retro fighting-game aesthetic inspired by late-1990s/early-2000s fighting games.

* Large, detailed pixel-art fighters
* Clearly visible individual pixels
* Limited but carefully chosen color palettes
* Strong silhouettes
* Hand-animated-looking sprite frames
* Slight sprite imperfections that make the game feel authentically retro
* Darker, dramatic arcade presentation
* Detailed backgrounds with lots of small environmental animation
* Retro UI, health bars, round indicators, character portraits, and combo counters
* Avoid smooth vector/cartoon graphics
* Avoid modern 3D mobile-game aesthetics
* Avoid overly exaggerated anime proportions
* Fighters should look like believable people with distinct personalities and fighting styles

The fighters should be visually recognizable as members of the **El Camino Real Math Department**, but exaggerated into fighting-game characters. Give each fighter a unique outfit, stance, personality, animations, and fighting style based loosely on their math-teacher identity.

## COMBAT PHILOSOPHY

The most important part of the game is the fighting.

Make the combat feel **heavy, precise, technical, and satisfying**, similar to classic Tekken.

Every attack should have:

* Startup frames
* Active frames
* Recovery frames
* Hit reaction
* Block reaction
* Hit stun
* Counter-hit behavior
* Different ranges
* Different knockback
* Appropriate sound effects
* Strong impact animation

Attacks should NOT look like characters simply waving their arms.

A punch should visibly involve the entire body:

* shoulder rotation
* hip rotation
* weight transfer
* foot movement
* realistic recovery

Kicks should similarly have believable balance, chambering, extension, impact, and recovery.

Use different animation timing for fast jabs, heavy punches, roundhouse kicks, low attacks, launcher attacks, throws, and defensive movements.

## MOVEMENT

Implement responsive fighting-game movement.

Include:

* Forward walk
* Backward walk
* Dash
* Backdash
* Crouch
* Sidestep
* Sidewalk
* Jump
* Quick recovery
* Turning animations
* Guard
* Crouching guard
* Attack interruption
* Knockdown recovery

Movement should have a slightly deliberate arcade feel rather than modern instant movement.

Backdashes and sidesteps should be useful defensive mechanics rather than cosmetic animations.

## COMBAT SYSTEM

Create a deep combo system.

Include:

* Light attacks
* Medium attacks
* Heavy attacks
* Low attacks
* Mid attacks
* High attacks
* Launchers
* Sweep attacks
* Counter hits
* Throws
* Air combos
* Wall combos
* Ground attacks
* Knockdowns
* Guard breaks
* Bound/bounce-style attacks
* Recovery mechanics

Allow players to discover their own combinations instead of forcing every combo into one predetermined sequence.

Example combo structure:

Jab → Jab → Mid → Launcher → Air Attack → Heavy Kick → Ground Follow-up

Also support more advanced combinations involving:

Sidestep → Counter Hit → Launcher → Dash → Air Combo

Low Attack → Mid Attack → Throw

Block → Punish → Launcher → Wall Combo

Make combos feel **earned and skill-based**.

Do not make every attack automatically connect.

## TEKKEN-INSPIRED FIGHTING FEEL

The game should capture the general feel of classic Tekken:

* Neutral game matters
* Spacing matters
* Whiff punishment matters
* Blocking matters
* Sidestepping matters
* Counter hits matter
* Frame advantage matters
* Launchers are powerful but punishable
* Heavy attacks feel dangerous
* Throws have readable animations
* Players should be able to learn character-specific combos

Do not directly copy Tekken characters, copyrighted animations, names, stages, or assets. Create an original fighting system inspired by the genre.

## IMPACT

Make attacks feel extremely satisfying.

When a powerful attack connects:

* Freeze the action for a tiny fraction of a second
* Add a strong impact frame
* Use a large pixel-art hit effect
* Add screen shake
* Play a deep impact sound
* Show a brief burst of particles
* Give the opponent a strong but believable hit reaction

Different attacks should have different impact styles.

A jab should feel quick.

A body shot should feel heavy.

A roundhouse should feel powerful.

A launcher should feel explosive.

A counter hit should feel especially dramatic.

Do NOT make every attack create giant explosions.

## CHARACTER DESIGN

Create a full roster of fictional fighters based around the **El Camino Real Math Department**.

Each character needs:

* Unique name
* Unique appearance
* Unique fighting style
* Unique stance
* Unique idle animation
* Unique movement animations
* Unique normals
* Unique special techniques
* Unique launcher
* Unique throw
* Unique combo routes
* Unique victory animation
* Unique defeat animation
* Unique personality

The math theme should be clever rather than ridiculous.

For example, attacks could have names such as:

* "Quadratic Counter"
* "Derivative Hook"
* "Integral Sweep"
* "Vector Rush"
* "Parabola Launcher"
* "Limit Break"
* "Prime Impact"
* "Function Feint"

However, these names should accompany REAL fighting animations rather than replacing them with magical math attacks.

## CHARACTER DIFFERENTIATION

Do not make every fighter play identically.

Create archetypes such as:

**Balanced Fighter**
Fast punches, reliable combos, average range.

**Technical Fighter**
Excellent sidesteps, counters, and difficult combo routes.

**Power Fighter**
Slow movement but extremely powerful attacks.

**Rushdown Fighter**
Fast pressure and aggressive strings.

**Defensive Fighter**
Excellent blocking, spacing, and punish attacks.

**Grappler**
Throws, command grabs, powerful close-range attacks.

**Tricky Fighter**
Unusual movement, stance transitions, feints, and mixups.

## ADVANCED ANIMATION

This is extremely important.

Do NOT use generic repeated punch animations.

Each fighter should have dozens of distinct attack animations.

Include:

* Different jab animations
* Straight punches
* Hooks
* Uppercuts
* Body punches
* Elbows
* Knee strikes
* Front kicks
* Side kicks
* Roundhouse kicks
* Low kicks
* Sweeps
* Spinning attacks
* Counter-hit animations
* Throws
* Throws from different directions
* Wake-up attacks
* Crouching attacks
* Sidestep attacks

Animations should have anticipation, impact, and recovery.

Characters should visibly react to their own movement and attacks.

## RETRO PRESENTATION

Make the game look like an extremely polished lost arcade fighting game from around 1998–2002.

Include:

* Pixel-art character portraits
* "ROUND 1"
* "READY"
* "FIGHT!"
* Health bars
* Timer
* Combo counter
* Character names
* Victory screen
* Arcade attract screen
* Character select screen
* Stage select screen

Use pixelated fonts and retro UI effects.

Keep the presentation stylish and serious rather than goofy.

## STAGES

Create several math-department-inspired fighting stages.

Examples:

**Math Classroom**
Whiteboards filled with equations, desks, calculators, posters, fluorescent lights, and students watching in the background.

**Math Department Hallway**
Lockers, classroom doors, bulletin boards, posters, vending machines, and fluorescent lighting.

**Computer Lab**
Old computers, CRT monitors, keyboards, cables, and math software displayed on screens.

**Outdoor Campus**
School buildings, trees, pavement, benches, and students in the distance.

**Department Office**
Desks, filing cabinets, bookshelves, papers, calculators, and mathematical posters.

Stages should have subtle animation and parallax depth.

## GAMEPLAY DETAILS

Include:

* Best-of-three rounds
* Round timer
* Character health
* Block meter or guard pressure
* Combo counter
* Counter-hit indicator
* Training mode
* Arcade mode
* Versus mode
* Character select
* Difficulty settings
* Practice dummy
* Frame/data display in training mode

Training mode should allow players to practice combos and understand why attacks connect or fail.

## IMPORTANT DESIGN RULE

The game should NOT feel like:

* A generic Roblox fighting game
* A button-mashing beat-em-up
* A mobile fighting game
* A cartoon brawler
* A simple sprite demo
* Fighters repeatedly throwing the same three punches
* Characters sliding around while attacking
* Characters floating during combos
* Weightless animations
* Giant unrealistic anime attacks

It SHOULD feel like:

**"What if an obscure, incredibly polished late-90s fighting game was made about the El Camino Real Math Department?"**

Prioritize **combat feel, animation quality, character identity, responsive controls, deep combos, satisfying impacts, and authentic retro presentation** above everything else.

Build the combat system so that even after seeing the game for five minutes, the player wants to keep experimenting with different attacks, movement options, counters, launchers, and combos.
